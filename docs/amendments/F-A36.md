# F-A36

Motion and touch R1-R5 (the "Canvas motion and touch" write-up, https://claude.ai/artifact/3tGxSd1bfBHmrRrjF5CMKD), with its two open questions taken as recommended.
**Tokens (R5)**: three durations, `MOTION` in `app.ts` and `--t-fast` 120 ms, `--t-mid` 200 ms, `--t-slow` 320 ms in `style.css`, with `--ease` and `--ease-exit`; every transition uses them, and `prefers-reduced-motion` sets them to 0.
A camera flight takes `--t-slow` (F-A26's 380 ms becomes 320 ms).
**Wheel (R1)**: a mouse notch with Ctrl or Meta (`deltaMode` other than pixels, or 50 px or more) eases to its zoom over `--t-fast`, and the notches that come during the ease add to its target; a trackpad pinch (small pixel steps) zooms at once, as before.
**Far look (R2)**: the swap to the snapshots comes on below zoom 0.42 and goes above 0.48 (0.45 only for the first frame), so a camera that rests near the line does not flip it; it waits while a hand, the wheel or a flight moves the camera and runs when it rests; it crossfades over `--t-fast`; on the way in the cards on screen fit at once and the rest in idle time, 12 at a time.
**Layout (R3)**: a layout change (tile, pack, compact, aspect, push apart, undo) is a FLIP: the cards are laid out at their end once and run there on a `transform` over `--t-slow`, not by transitioning left, top, width and height.
A new card enters (fade and scale from 96 % over `--t-mid`); a removed card leaves a ghost that fades over `--t-fast`.
**Hit areas (R4)**: on a coarse pointer the header buttons, a folder tab's buttons, the ports and the resize handles have invisible hit areas of at least 44 CSS px on screen.
**Touch**: one finger on a card scrolls the card (as before); a double tap with one finger zooms 2x about the finger on bare canvas and fits a folder on its own area, and makes no page (the long-press menu has "New page here", F-A34).
Two fingers in an artifact card pinch the board's camera, and so do one in an artifact and one on the canvas: `bridge.js` reports the page's fingers (`{t:'touch', phase, touches}`, frame CSS pixels) and the board holds them in its pinch as pointers of their own; while the board pinches with a page's finger it tells that page (`{t:'pinch', on}`), which then prevents its own `touchmove`.
A web card is another origin and cannot report: its first finger stays the page's (F-A26)
